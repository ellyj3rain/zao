## Record

This repository had no `CREDITS.md`. It has read five other mods for
evidence across `[A6]`, `[A7]` and `[A8]`, names them in findings and
in decisions, and had nowhere that stated what it takes from any of
them - which for every one of them is nothing.

The file exists now, and two operator rulings that were being applied
without being written down are written down.

## DR-014, and why the file is short

The operator's ruling: the value is not in acquiring other people's
systems. Copying a mod produces a worse copy of that mod. What does not
exist anywhere is the substrate that lets disparate good work cohere
while this project's own systems carry the causation.

The Workshop already answers narrow questions well - what a special
infected does, how a hostile fights, how an infection course runs, how
a companion takes an order - and every answer ships with its own small
world-model attached: its own way of marking a body, its own taxonomy,
its own reason a population exists. Four good mods in one load order
produce four unrelated ontologies in one county.

What is built here is the county those answers can be true inside at
once. Complementary work becomes an expression on this project's own
people rather than a second population beside them.

**What is cheap and what is expensive.** A first pass at this batch
kept arguing about other mods' taxonomies - which named types this
project would and would not adopt. That is the wrong axis. Names,
categories and a mod's own account of why a body is the way it is are
the cheapest part of it and cost nothing to discard. Working code that
makes a body leap a fence, charge in a line, resist a bullet or cry
where it sits is months of craft. This project is indifferent to the
first and interested in the second: a capability gets renamed,
recombined and re-caused freely, and what decides which body has it is
always this project's own account of who that person was. The goal the
whole thing answers to is a better game than anyone is currently
providing.

So `CREDITS.md` is mostly a list of things taken from nobody. That is
the correct shape for it and not an omission.

## DR-015, at the sharpest case

Bandits is the case that makes the general rule concrete. Its shapes
are useful and its production is not: a mod that reliably manufactures
hostiles answers a different question from the one this project asks,
and a hostile that was placed destroys the only measurement there is.
It is the sister's DR-037 applied to hostility instead of to houses.

Bandits is a compatibility target and prior art. The half of the ruling
that belongs to the living county is the sister's.

## What each source actually says, which is not one thing

The first draft of `CREDITS.md` wrote *no licence stated - removed on
request* against five of six entries. That is a false dichotomy and it
was wrong on the facts, because it was written after searching the mod
trees for licence files and before reading four of the Workshop pages
at all. The positions differ:

| Source | What it actually states |
|---|---|
| ZombieBuddy | **MIT**, (c) 2025, licence text shipped in the mod |
| Antibodies | **MIT** on the public repository; the Workshop page states nothing |
| Bandits | copyrighted, no reupload without written permission, not authorised for posting on Steam except by the author's own account |
| The Mutants | nothing stated on the page or in the tree; its sounds carry ElevenLabs' terms |
| Knox Survivors | nothing stated on the page; the public launcher repository declares none |
| Special Zombies Framework | nothing stated on the page |

Two of the six are MIT, which was not known when `[A6]` and `[A7]`
were written. **Antibodies being MIT bears directly on the decision
`[A6]` handed the operator**: F-011 left open whether naming a mod in
code is acceptable when the only door in is a named one, and a mod
under MIT can be read as source rather than named at runtime. That does
not decide the fork - it adds an option it did not have.

Bandits' statement is a restriction on redistribution and reposting.
This project redistributes none of it, so the posture in DR-015 sits
inside what the author asks.

## The error, because it is the same one all day

Five times in one session an absence was asserted from a narrow search:
`known[...] = nil`, `javap` from a path with no `javap`, a `mod.info`
scan with a depth limit that hid its own subject, "Bandits publishes
nothing", and now "no licence stated" across five mods whose pages had
not been opened. The catalog carries all five. The shape is fixed:
searching one place, finding nothing, and writing a claim about
everywhere.
